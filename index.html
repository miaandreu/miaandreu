About & study

A-101–102

I'm the oldest of three, and I've loved building things for as long as I can remember. My favorite kind of problem is a puzzle I can work from start to finish. That mix of art and problem-solving brought me to NYU Tandon, where I study mechanical engineering with a focus on sustainable cities and communities.

Cities keep me because the constraints are real: code requirements, budgets, physics, and the people who live with the result. The solutions that work hold up under all of them at once. And the ones that last are the ones communities understand, which is why teaching matters to me as much as building. That's the kind of problem I want to keep working on.

Outside the lab or a job site, I'm probably knitting, lifting, or wandering a museum.

My global challenge ties two UN goals together: Sustainable Cities and Communities (SDG 11) and Quality Education (SDG 4). Cities only get more sustainable when the people in them can learn, build, and lead.

WSP and Piedmont showed me how much energy commercial buildings waste. Hanoi showed me how one intersection shapes the air a whole neighborhood breathes. Teaching in Antigua and tutoring in Durham showed me what a classroom can change. Milan and Hanoi taught me that solutions have to be designed with communities, not just for them.
